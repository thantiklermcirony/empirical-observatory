# A living Observatory: stable evidence, revisable explanations

10 September 2026. Implemented capabilities and proposed next stages are distinguished throughout.

The programme asks what a description must remember to predict or act well. That question connects state, observation, intervention, composition, recovery and uncertainty. Its ambition spans science. Its value must be earned through specific proofs, useful instruments and experiments that survive stronger alternatives.

The most promising product is an **observatory of descriptions**. Visitors enter a problem, inspect the representation, expose what it hides and try an observation that could distinguish competing explanations. A useful outcome may be a repair, an improved prediction, a failed hypothesis, or a clearly identified missing measurement. The interface should make each outcome traceable.

## The centre

There should be no unchallengeable mathematical or philosophical centre. The current organising question can be revised if another framing produces better research. What must remain stable during a comparison is the declared experimental contract: available information, outcomes, costs, baselines, acceptance rule and data separation. Changing that contract creates a new version, with the old result still accessible.

This is a distinction between principles of accountable inquiry and the scientific content being investigated. Source records, context, uncertainty, failed tests and traceable versions protect the inquiry. They do not make any particular geometry, scalar, state representation or manuscript immune from challenge.

## Exploration as a working space

The current Atlas is a functional perspective graph. Dragging rotates a three-dimensional arrangement projected onto the screen; keyboard controls and a text index provide equivalent access. Selecting a line opens its question, evidence, limits, sources and next discriminating test. The arrangement is editorial navigation, not a measured geometry or a literal hologram.

The next spatial instruments should each expose a specific mathematical distinction:

| Instrument | What the visitor changes | What the display must compute | Present status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Programme Atlas | Research line, layer, viewpoint | Typed links and evidence dossier | Implemented |
| Adaptive exploration | Measurement policy or manual next test | Likelihood update, cost, recommendation, conclusion and failure | Implemented in a four-state simulation |
| Virtual Cell | Held-out context, response target and amplitude | Recorded prediction, published response and comparable error | Implemented first flight; candidate did not pass |
| State microscope | Which history/context coordinates are retained | Whether previously merged histories have distinguishable future outcomes | Proposed extension with explicit future-test family |
| Composition bench | Order, grouping, coordinate generator and calibration | Order versus reassociation residuals under a declared operation | Existing TAO/quantum examples; general instrument proposed |
| Recovery field | Intervention sequence and remaining action budget | Reachable recovery and loss of feasible actions | Synthetic TAO control exists; general viability instrument proposed |
| Hypothesis comparison room | Model assumption and fresh challenge | Matched predictions, uncertainty, compute cost and falsifiers | Proposed shared workflow |

Visual intensity must not imply evidence strength. The geometry in a scientific display should derive from stated quantities; changing coordinates should preserve the invariants it claims to illustrate. When the display is only a navigation arrangement, it must say so. Every visually impressive feature needs a corresponding experiment or learning purpose.

## How conclusions should move

The intended lifecycle is **proposal → declared test → observation → scoped conclusion → challenge → versioned revision**. A conclusion contains its source, measurement units, context, assumptions, outcome, uncertainty, comparison and failure condition. A new observation can contradict it without deleting the original record.

The current implementation has replayable local simulation notebooks and reviewed public releases. It does not yet have a shared claim database, automatic literature ingestion, collaborative adjudication or unattended laboratory control. Visitors' local records do not become published scientific evidence when saved.

A later shared ledger should make claims, observations, methods, datasets and implementations separate objects. Connections should specify formal dependence, an empirical test, implementation, hypothesis or analogy. Multiple manuscripts can use one evidence object without counting it repeatedly as independent support. A failed replication should attach to the relevant claim and scope, not disappear into a general project log.

## What evolves

1. **Beliefs within a run:** implemented. New measurements revise probabilities.
2. **The next experiment within a run:** implemented for the finite simulator. The current information-gain rule chooses from declared tests under a fixed budget.
3. **An exploration policy across runs:** not implemented. It requires learning and independent comparison on new problems, including costs and failure cases.
4. **Scientific representations and models:** currently changed through explicit human/agent research and reviewed releases. A proposed state refinement must improve prospective prediction or action, rather than merely redescribe a known failure.
5. **The programme's philosophical framing:** open to reasoned revision. Breadth alone is not an acceptance test.

An evolving observatory need not autonomously rewrite its source. It first needs a reliable way to propose, compare and retain changes. Automatic adoption becomes reasonable only after the comparison mechanism itself is tested and its authority is bounded.

## Three projects to carry forward

**First: turn context into a useful biological warning.** The next Virtual Cell study asks whether controls and training-source evidence predict when transfer will fail in unseen cell lines. A 50-cell-line chemical-assay control panel is now audited and packaged. Freeze the remaining model and scale contract before opening response values; test risk at fixed coverage against ordinary disagreement and expression baselines. A failure should narrow the next hypothesis.

**Second: build the state microscope.** Use the existing memory, timing and controller-recovery examples as separate fixtures. Deliberately hide one observable distinction, record which future predictions become inconsistent and compare a proposed memory refinement with simple history buffers and conventional state estimators. The test is whether the method finds useful distinctions prospectively on fresh cases at a stated cost.

**Third: evaluate an exploration policy across problems.** Provide a bounded family of finite systems with varied noise, costs and failure objectives. Train on one partition, freeze policy and budget, then compare on untouched systems against random, fixed, information-gain and decision-oriented policies. Include worlds where the candidate should lose. Only a measured gain here would support a limited claim of learned self-improvement; transfer to real laboratories is a later experiment.

These projects turn the broad idea into reproducible work other people can use, challenge and extend. A compelling public demonstration combines a visible problem, a small intervention, an honest before/after comparison and a one-command reproduction. Interest is an outcome to measure; it cannot substitute for scientific success.

## Current evidence and limits

The recurring pattern across Graphiti history, NeuroGym phase cues and Ray controller identity is that an operationally important distinction was missing or stale. Those repairs are useful engineering contributions. Pertpy and Virtual Cell make baseline and context checks concrete. The first Virtual Cell candidate's negative result is informative about that candidate, not a victory over the field.

UHL, IDA, TAO, adaptation, ecology, AI, physical foundations and arithmetic retain separate proof and empirical obligations. Similar nouns and verbs help organise research; they do not establish identical mechanisms. The [programme audit](Programme_Audit.md) maps those obligations without assuming all manuscripts are independently verified or all contributions have been adopted upstream.

The Atlas and adaptive loop are an implemented first version of this architecture. The shared ledger, learned exploration policy and general state microscope remain proposed work.
